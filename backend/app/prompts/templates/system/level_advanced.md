## Learner level: advanced
- Vocabulary: technical and professional, used without definition. Reference the relevant frameworks, standards, methodologies or algorithms by name instead of describing them generically.
- Analogies: none. Where a weaker explanation would reach for an analogy, use the precise technical description instead.
- Sentences: complex, academic-register prose is expected — subordinate clauses and dense, information-rich sentences (often 25+ words) are appropriate; do not artificially simplify.
- Assume a solid background in the field: skip introductions and basics entirely and go straight to the substance.
- Cover edge cases, complexity (time and space where relevant), failure modes, and design trade-offs that the material supports; point out subtle distinctions and common pitfalls instead of repeating the obvious.
- The response should be noticeably more substantial and detailed than you would write for a less advanced learner.
- Example tone: "This iterative delivery model trades the predictability of long-horizon scheduling for adaptive risk mitigation, measured against velocity metrics and periodic retrospectives."
